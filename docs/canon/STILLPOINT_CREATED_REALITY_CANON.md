Document Purpose
This document is the canonical reference layer beneath the master essay. It does not replace 
StillPoint: The Grammar of Created Reality; it makes that essay operationally legible by defining 
terms, relationships, boundaries, source levels, protected formulations, and the conditions under 
which canon may be reopened. Its purpose is to prevent later summaries, software 
implementations, policy applications, research briefs, sermons, or derivative writing from 
shrinking the theology into a theory of government, a calendar preference, a generic doctrine of 
limits, or a metaphor about rest.
The architecture is frozen as of September 28, 2026. Frozen does not mean immune from truth. It 
means current work must treat the architecture as settled enough to build until contradiction, 
stronger evidence, mathematical failure, source failure, or theological failure gives a specific reason
to reopen it. Editorial improvement, reformatting, implementation convenience, institutional 
preference, or a new slogan is not sufficient warrant to change canon.

1. Governing Ontology: Source and Reception
StillPoint begins with the claim that God is Source. God does not receive being from a prior reality, 
institution, process, calendar, law, or creature. Created reality does. To be created is therefore to 
receive existence rather than originate it. This is the first distinction and the root of every later 
jurisdictional claim.
Reception is not inferiority. A received thing can be fully real, genuinely active, morally 
responsible, creative, authoritative, and capable of relationship without becoming self-originating. 
The architecture rejects the serpent’s equation of dependence with humiliation. Creaturehood is 
not the defect salvation eventually removes; it is the good form in which created life belongs to God.
Because existence is received, every creaturely capacity is downstream of gift. Time, body, place, 
knowledge, power, office, memory, property, skill, identity, and relationship all occur within a 
reality the creature did not create. This does not invalidate creaturely participation; it sets the 
conditions under which participation can remain truthful.
The practical consequence is that no creaturely achievement can retroactively become sourcehood. 
A person may build, discover, inherit, govern, teach, heal, write, or create; all of those acts are real 
participation. None of them make the participant the origin of the reality in which the act became 
possible. StillPoint therefore treats gratitude, accountability, and release as ontological 
consequences of reception rather than optional moral decoration.
2. Distinction, Boundary, and Relation
Distinction is pre-Fall and good. Genesis does not present boundary as a punishment added after 
sin. Creation itself is made through separations, names, domains, and ordered differences. Light is 

not darkness; sea is not land; Creator is not creature. These distinctions are not failures of unity. 
They are the conditions under which particular realities can exist and relate.
A boundary is therefore more than a prohibition. It protects meaningful otherness. If one reality 
entirely absorbs another, relation collapses into possession. Communion requires distinction 
because communion requires more than one reality genuinely present. This is the deeper reason 
relation sits underneath jurisdiction in the architecture.
Canonical rule: distinction without separation. Something can come from another, belong to 
another, be shaped by another, or remain covenantally bound to another without becoming that 
other or becoming its possession. The project’s phrase “From does not mean separate” protects 
origin and relation simultaneously.
This protects StillPoint from becoming a philosophy in which every boundary is automatically 
good. Boundaries themselves must remain answerable to the relation they are supposed to protect. 
A boundary that annihilates the other, prevents all correction, or exists only to preserve unilateral 
possession has contradicted the reason boundary matters in the first place.
From does not mean separate. Belonging does not require absorption.
3. Jurisdiction and Sovereignty
Jurisdiction is a real, received field of action whose validity does not make it total. A jurisdiction 
answers what may be done, by whom, within what field, on what warrant, for what purpose, and 
until what endpoint. The concept is older than law because creation itself distributes fields of action
before any human institution exists.
Sovereignty, in the strongest theological sense used here, belongs to Source. It is not simply very 
large jurisdiction. It is the position from which the field itself is not received from a higher 
creaturely authority. Creatures may exercise authority, but their authority remains derivative, 
bounded, answerable, and capable of completion.
Real authority does not require total authority. In fact, creaturely authority becomes illegitimate 
when it destroys the otherness it was meant to serve. A ruler who turns citizens into extensions of 
the state, a parent who turns adulthood into perpetual childhood, or a church that claims 
ownership of a believer’s relation to God has exceeded jurisdiction by collapsing relation into 
possession.
Jurisdiction therefore has positive and negative dimensions. Positively, it gives a creature room to 
act genuinely. Negatively, it prevents the creature from treating participation as sourcehood. A 
valid field can be wide and still finite; the size of the field never changes its ontological status.
4. Seizure and Possession
Seizure is the conversion of received jurisdiction into a claim of sovereignty. In the garden, the 
creature does not simply desire a fruit; the deeper movement is the claim to determine whether the
given boundary continues to bind. The receiver attempts to become the source of the terms of 
reception.
Possession is the relational form of seizure. To possess in the problematic sense is not merely to 
have, steward, own, use, or care for something. It is to claim that the other has no meaningful 

existence outside the possessor’s continuing title over it. Possession turns a relation into an 
extension.
Possession may grow from a lie, but the more difficult case is possession born from truth. A real 
office becomes permanent title. A real wound becomes sovereignty over every future relationship. 
A real diagnosis becomes permanent identity. A real parental relation becomes ownership of 
adulthood. A real historical fact becomes a prohibition on new evidence.
This is why StillPoint pays unusual attention to successful descriptions and legitimate authority. 
The system is not designed only to stop obvious lies. It is designed to stop finite truth from acquiring
infinite jurisdiction simply because it began as truth.
Possession frequently begins not with a lie but with a truth that refuses to stop.
5. Jurisdictional and Temporal Non-Inheritance
Jurisdictional non-inheritance is the rule that a valid claim in one domain does not silently acquire 
authority in another domain. Competence in one field does not create competence everywhere. 
Knowledge does not create permission. Harm suffered does not create ownership of another 
person. A successful action does not authorize a different action. A theological claim does not self￾execute as software authority.
Temporal non-inheritance is the corresponding rule across time. What was true then does not 
automatically govern now. A prior current-state description may become history while losing 
present jurisdiction. A past warrant may expire. A completed office may remain honorable while 
no longer active. An old diagnosis may remain in the record while no longer defining the current 
state.
The two errors often combine. A prior truth is carried forward in time and then extended into a 
new domain. StillPoint requires each extension to earn its own warrant. A claim must answer both 
where it applies and how long it applies.
These rules are not anti-tradition, anti-memory, or anti-authority. They protect tradition, memory, 
and authority from becoming counterfeit sovereignty. A tradition can remain authoritative inside 
its field without pretending that duration alone proves every later extension. A memory can remain
accurate without becoming the person. Authority can remain real without inheriting infinity.
You proved X; you are spending X as if it were Y.
6. Sabbath, Completion, and Release
Sabbath is the recurring temporal interruption of creaturely seizure. Six days authorize genuine 
action; the seventh refuses the conversion of action into sovereignty. Sabbath is not primarily a 
productivity technique. It is creation returning to the truth that work can be real without becoming 
infinite.
Completion is not failure. A thing may reach its proper end without becoming meaningless. God’s 
rest in Genesis establishes that stopping can belong to holiness. The Source can complete work and 
remain Source; creation can remain creation. This is the first canonical answer to the fear that 
release equals disappearance.

Release is the end of a finite claim’s rightful jurisdiction without erasing the truth, history, relation, 
or value that preceded it. Release therefore differs from abandonment and from forgetting. It 
preserves what was real while refusing possession of what comes next.
Release also differs from defeat. A ruler may leave office because the office was successfully 
completed, a parent may release a child because maturation succeeded, and a teacher may release 
a student because teaching worked. StillPoint rejects the assumption that only perpetual 
continuation can prove that earlier work mattered.
Release is not abandonment. Release is what prevents relation from becoming 
possession.
7. Torah, Sabbatical Structure, and Jubilee
Torah extends the Sabbath architecture from the individual week into land, economy, debt, labor, 
and generations. The seventh-day cessation becomes a social pattern: human authority must 
contain real conditions under which its claim stops, releases, returns, or is reviewed.
The sabbatical and Jubilee materials should be read with textual precision. Leviticus 25 and 
Deuteronomy 15 contain related but distinct arrangements; StillPoint does not flatten them into a 
single legal code. What matters structurally is that land, debt, servitude, and inheritance remain 
under a prior divine claim and therefore cannot simply become infinite creaturely title.
Jubilee is especially important because it addresses duration. An arrangement can survive long 
enough to feel natural and still remain finite. Time does not turn creaturely possession into 
divinity.
The canonical principle is therefore not that every social claim must end on the same schedule. It is 
that legitimate creaturely claims must remain finite, reviewable, and answerable to a prior reality. 
Sabbath, sabbatical release, and Jubilee are scriptural witnesses to that deeper structure.
8. Temporal Architecture and the 364-Day Witness
Genesis gives the grammar of day, evening and morning, sevenfold sequence, heavenly lights, and 
seventh-day completion. The wider Second Temple witness supplies explicit 364-day forms. Jubilees
6 presents a 364-day reckoning, and Qumran calendrical materials include related fixed-year 
structures. 4Q252 explicitly describes a complete year of 364 days.
Mathematically, 364 days contain exactly fifty-two seven-day weeks. Ninety-one days contain 
exactly thirteen weeks, allowing four equal quarters. The theological significance assigned by 
StillPoint is structural: the larger temporal cycle can preserve the integrity of the smaller cycle 
instead of fracturing it.
The project’s current Common Calendar implementation is a separate CURRENT CANON layer. It 
uses an immutable 364-date annual surface with fifty-two complete weeks. That implementation 
must not be confused with the ancient sources themselves. Source, interpretation, synthesis, 
current canon, and implementation remain distinct levels of authority.
The calendrical witness is therefore neither disposable decoration nor the foundation of every 
theological claim. The ontology does not stand or fall on a single manuscript. At the same time, the 

364-day tradition gives concrete temporal form to the seven-preserving architecture and remains 
an important part of the working canon.
9. Weekly StillPoint and Local Light Events
The recurring weekly StillPoint architecture runs from local Friday sunset through Sabbath to local 
Sunday sunrise. The window is intentionally not defined by a fixed number of clock hours; local 
light conditions vary. Sunset, darkness, dawn, sunrise, and daylight are observational creation￾events layered over the fixed civil calendar rather than mechanisms that rewrite the civil grid.
The weekly window and the historical Passion interval are structurally related but not identical 
clock measurements. Jesus dies and is buried before Sabbath; the Gospels narrate the discovery of 
the empty tomb at the opening of the first day without timestamping the instant of resurrection. 
The weekly form therefore holds the Sabbath-centered passage of cessation, boundary, and re￾entry; the supreme historical form is Christ’s actual death-to-resurrection passage.
StillPoint is thus fundamentally a jurisdictional passage rather than a quantity of hours. The 
calendar witnesses and disciplines the passage; it does not manufacture its meaning.
This distinction is protected canon because collapsing the two intervals creates unnecessary 
historical error. The weekly form may be computed from local light events. The Passion interval 
must remain faithful to what the Gospel texts actually give and what they leave unstated.
10. Creaturehood and the Natural Order
StillPoint is the shape of creaturehood before it is a theory of institutions. Everything created 
receives being and therefore receives its capacity, place, time, and field of action. Finitude is not 
identical to worthlessness. The creature is good precisely as a creature.
Natural and biological rhythms may be used as disciplined analogies: sleep and waking, repair and 
exertion, cellular checkpoints, seasonal dormancy, growth regulation, and organism-level 
constraints all show that created life is not obviously improved by escaping form. These 
observations do not prove StillPoint theology and must not be cited as if science had verified 
Sabbath doctrine.
The canonical boundary is methodological: evidence from biology may illuminate the plausibility of
bounded life, but physics and biology do not become theological SOURCE. StillPoint must never 
launder theological claims through scientific vocabulary merely to make them sound more 
objective.
11. Christology: The Uncreated Son Enters Created Life
The eternal Son is not a creature. The architecture therefore must never say that “everything except
God receives being” and then treat Christ as simply another created receiver. Christian confession 
holds that the One who is with God and is God assumes genuine created human existence without 
ceasing to be who he is.
The incarnation becomes a supreme instance of distinction without separation. Divinity does not 
absorb humanity; humanity is not discarded so divinity can remain intact. The Son receives body, 
time, people, hunger, vulnerability, obedience, suffering, and death as genuinely human realities.

Christ therefore answers Adam not by escaping creaturehood but by inhabiting human reception 
without seizure. Philippians 2 is read within that structure: equality with God is not converted into 
self-serving exploitation; the movement is toward self-giving, service, obedience, and death.
This Christological guardrail is non-negotiable because the entire architecture depends on the 
difference between Source and creature. The incarnation reveals the goodness of creaturely 
reception; it does not erase the ontological distinction between the uncreated Son and created 
human existence.
12. Passion, Tomb, and Resurrection
The Passion carries the architecture through the hardest created boundary. Death is allowed to be 
real. The body dies, is buried, and remains in the tomb. StillPoint does not protect resurrection by 
pretending death was illusion, sleep, or metaphor.
The tomb is the place where the previous order has exhausted its ability to continue itself. No 
disciple, institution, state, priesthood, ritual, or body can manufacture resurrection. This is why the 
interval matters: completion is given time to become real before newness arrives.
Resurrection is gift again. It is not Friday restarted. It is continuity with the crucified one without 
captivity to crucifixion. The Source remains capable of giving after a created condition reaches 
completion. Completion cannot exhaust God.
The occupied tomb and the empty tomb therefore have different evidentiary roles. The occupied 
tomb witnesses a real condition. The empty tomb witnesses that the condition no longer governs 
the present. The same history remains; current jurisdiction changes.
Completion cannot exhaust God.
13. Temporal Truth and Continuing Evidence
Temporal truth distinguishes historical truth from current-state truth. “Jesus died” remains true as 
history. “Jesus is dead” is true only while death remains the current condition. Resurrection does 
not falsify the first sentence; it changes whether the second sentence corresponds to reality.
A description can therefore be perfectly true and still lose jurisdiction over the present. The 
governing principle is not relativism. Truth remains answerable to reality. What changes is the 
thing described, and therefore any present-tense description must remain reachable by later 
evidence.
Systems faithful to StillPoint must preserve two fidelities: fidelity to what happened and fidelity to 
what is happening. Destroy the first and the system becomes amnesiac. Destroy the second and the 
system becomes carceral.
Continuing evidence is the practical answer. A successful description must remain correctable by 
later reality. The more consequential the authority based on the description, the more important it 
is that the description carry observation time, effective time, review conditions, and a path for 
supersession.
StillPoint does not limit truth. It limits the jurisdiction of a true description over 
changing reality.

14. Lazarus, Prodigal, and Vineyard: Canonical Demonstrations
Lazarus demonstrates temporal non-inheritance. “Lazarus died” remains history; “Lazarus is dead”
ceases to describe the present after he is called out. The grave clothes belonged to a prior true state. 
“Unbind him and let him go” becomes the canonical image of releasing a binding whose underlying
state has changed.
The older brother in Luke 15 demonstrates a temporal identity error. The younger son’s waste 
remains true, but the older brother attempts to spend a true was as though it permanently 
authorizes is. The father preserves history without allowing history to own the restored 
relationship.
The vineyard workers demonstrate jurisdictional non-inheritance. “We worked longer” is true, but 
the truth about one worker’s labor never contained jurisdiction over the landowner’s generosity 
toward another.
Together the three stories distinguish three questions: what happened, what is true now, and what 
domain a truth actually governs. StillPoint requires those questions to remain distinguishable.
15. Memory Without Captivity
Redemption does not require a pastless person. It requires that the past cease pretending to be 
Source. Memory, evidence, scar, record, and consequence may remain while their authority is 
bounded by present reality.
The canonical resurrection image is: the wounds remain; the nails do not. The marks preserve 
continuity with crucifixion. The instrument of captivity no longer holds the body. History remains 
embodied without remaining master.
This principle governs personal identity, institutional memory, criminal and administrative 
records, diagnosis, software state, and theological interpretation. Preservation of history is not 
permission for history to own the future.
Memory without captivity therefore rejects both erasure and imprisonment. It keeps enough 
history to tell the truth and enough openness to receive the person, institution, or reality as it now 
is.
The wounds remain; the nails do not.
16. Canonical Definition of StillPoint
The phrase created boundary means the boundary belongs to the structure of received reality 
rather than being merely an institutional policy invented later. Finite jurisdiction means the 
preceding claim may be real and legitimate without becoming sovereign. Ends means authority 
reaches an actual boundary rather than remaining indefinitely dormant but revivable without new
warrant. What it could never own means the released reality was never ontologically the property 
of the finite authority, even when that authority had a legitimate relation to it. Becomes available to
relation again means release is not abandonment; the other can now be encountered without the 
old claim of possession.

StillPoint therefore protects continuity from becoming captivity. It gives authority a way to stop 
without requiring memory to disappear, relation to be severed, or prior meaning to become false.
StillPoint is the created boundary where finite jurisdiction ends and what it could 
never own becomes available to relation again.
17. Love, Communion, and Jurisdictional Restraint
If possession destroys relation, love itself requires jurisdictional restraint. “I love you” cannot mean
“therefore I have unlimited jurisdiction over you.” Love that cannot tolerate meaningful otherness 
becomes possession even when its language remains affectionate.
This applies to parents, spouses, pastors, teachers, institutions, states, and communities. Care may 
create genuine responsibility; it does not create infinite title. The measure of mature love is not 
merely intensity but whether the beloved can remain real outside the lover’s control.
The theological foundation is divine creation itself. God gives room to what is not God. StillPoint 
therefore treats release not as the opposite of love but as one of the ways love refuses to become 
possession.

19. Source Discipline and Epistemic Levels
StillPoint uses a five-level epistemic discipline. SOURCE is what an artifact, text, observation, or 
external evidence actually says or shows. HISTORICAL REALITY is the world and context in which 
the source existed. INTERPRETATION is constrained reasoning from that evidence. SYNTHESIS is 
the StillPoint arrangement connecting multiple sources or phenomena. REALITY-LEVEL CLAIM is 
the larger ontological assertion that the recurring structure belongs to reality itself.
For implementation governance, the shorter authority chain is SOURCE interpretation/synthesis →
→ → CURRENT CANON implementation. Software may encode CURRENT CANON; software cannot 
promote its own behavior into SOURCE. A test passing does not prove theology. A historical source 
does not self-execute as policy. A theological canon does not create a warrant.
When evidence challenges canon, the correction path must be explicit. A source conflict, 
contradiction, mathematical failure, or stronger evidence may trigger reconsideration. 
Convenience and implementation inertia may not.
The source discipline also protects the project from false modesty. If a source plainly says 
something, it should not be reduced to “Robert’s interpretation” merely to avoid making a factual 
claim. Source and synthesis must be distinguished without erasing either.
20. Canon Reopening Protocol
1. Identify the exact canonical proposition allegedly in tension with new evidence.
2. Classify the new material: SOURCE, historical evidence, interpretation, synthesis, mathematical 
result, observational evidence, or implementation behavior.
3. Show the contradiction, counterexample, or failure explicitly. A mere alternative formulation is 
not enough.
4. Determine scope: local clarification, definition change, calendar rule change, Christological 
correction, temporal-authority correction, or architectural revision.
5. Preserve unaffected canon. No global rewrite is authorized by a local failure.
6. Record the decision, rationale, superseded wording, effective date, and downstream 
implementation surfaces.
7. Require deliberate re-ratification before implementation mutates frozen canon.
21. What StillPoint Is Not
 Not merely a civic theory about how institutions should limit themselves.
 Not merely a metaphor for rest, liminality, grief, or transition.
 Not a claim that every natural rhythm scientifically proves Sabbath theology.
 Not a mechanism that guarantees resurrection or new beginnings when a creature performs 
release correctly.
 Not an argument that history should be erased, records deleted, or accountability suspended 
whenever someone changes.
 Not a doctrine that every boundary is good. Boundaries themselves remain answerable to 
Source, truth, relation, and legitimate jurisdiction.
 Not permission for software, clergy, governments, parents, experts, or institutions to claim 
theological sovereignty.

 Not an identification of the weekly Friday-sunset-to-Sunday-sunrise window with a precise 
Gospel timestamp for resurrection.
 Not a claim that the modern implementation of the 364-day calendar is itself an ancient SOURCE
document.
22. Canonical Closing
The architecture closes where it began. God is Source; therefore created reality is gift. Because 
created reality is received, created authority is real without being sovereign. Because relation 
requires distinction, boundary is not merely restriction but the condition of communion. Because 
finite authority can become possession, creation requires completion and release. Sabbath 
rehearses that truth. Torah carries it into social order. The calendar makes its sevenfold temporal 
architecture countable. Christ enters it without seizure. Death receives a real jurisdiction but not 
infinity. Resurrection reveals that no created condition owns what God may yet give.
The future remains God’s, not because the past was unreal, but because nothing created acquires 
God’s right to determine reality forever.
