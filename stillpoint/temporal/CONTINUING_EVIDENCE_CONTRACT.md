# Continuing Evidence Contract

StillPoint distinguishes truth, memory, and authority.

The governing rule is:

> Every finite exercise of authority must remain answerable to the conditions that authorize its continuation.

A Claim Envelope does **not** grant authority. It preserves the conditions under which a claim may remain operationally relevant: purpose, domain, epistemic reach, permitted uses, continuation conditions, correction routes, release conditions, re-entry requirements, and memory policy.

## Required separations

- A claim may remain true after authority to act on it ends.
- Memory may preserve a former claim without reauthorizing it.
- Operational use of a claim must be linked to the warrant and action that used it.
- If system use of a claim changes the environment, later evidence must be capable of being marked post-intervention or mixed rather than being treated as independent confirmation.
- A historical or superseded envelope is **former**: preserved as history, presently non-governing.
- Failure of a continuation condition moves operational reliance to review; it does not erase history.
- Passing the Claim Envelope checks establishes scope/continuation only. Consequential action still requires the ordinary StillPoint warrant gate.

## Non-inheritance

Competence may compound. Authority does not silently compound with it.

A successful claim, model, worker, reviewer, or institution does not acquire new jurisdiction merely because prior work was accurate. Continued authority must remain tied to present conditions, and re-entry after release requires present standing rather than resurrection of old title.
